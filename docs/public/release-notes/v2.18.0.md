## v2.18.0 - October 8, 2026

Schedules can now belong to a factory seat and can fire at random times on a human rhythm, the Mac installer no longer leaves a refused launcher behind, and prompts are no longer refused because of Claude Code's grey suggestion.

### Highlights
- Schedules that belong to a factory. `cc-devthrottle schedule create` takes `--factory` and `--seat`, and
  `cc-devthrottle schedule link` attaches an existing schedule to a factory seat. A schedule names both a factory and
  a seat, or neither, and the seat must be registered - otherwise the command refuses with a sentence naming the fix
  and nothing is stored. A schedule linked to a factory or seat the registry does not have will not fire. Where the
  factory area is turned on, the Factories screen lists every enabled schedule that belongs to no factory under
  "Schedules outside any factory".
- Random schedules. A third kind of schedule beside recurring and one-off: about N fires a day at random times inside
  a daily window, kept a minimum gap apart, weighted by default toward the hours a person is usually active. Create
  one with `cc-devthrottle schedule create` and `--random <window> --per-day <N> --min-gap <minutes>`, and see the
  times still to come with `cc-devthrottle schedule plan <id>`. The times are worked out the same way every time, so
  a restart does not change them. The Cockpit shows the schedule in words and today's remaining times.
- The Mac install fixes a refused launcher. Every install on a Mac now rebuilds the launcher's launch agent, and the
  Director repairs a launcher that macOS has refused to start. A launcher that still will not start no longer fails
  the install: the Director is installed and opened, the launcher line reads Warning, and the last screen says what
  losing autostart means.
- Prompts are no longer refused because of Claude Code's grey suggestion. Claude Code shows a faint guess at your
  next prompt in its empty input box. The Director read that guess as text you had typed, so a short prompt such as
  "go" could be refused as "not delivered", and an idle session could keep putting off its waiting messages. The
  Director now ignores faint text when it reads the input box.

### Also in this release
- A prompt that was not delivered now shows one small red "Not delivered" chip instead of two large red blocks. Click
  it to see the reason, the words that were not delivered, and Send anyway, Retry or Dismiss. This is in the Cockpit
  and the mobile app.
- A session that asks to talk to another session now shows that as a badge on the asking session, with Approve and
  Deny in its header, instead of a full-width card. The session says how much talking it needs, and Approve allows
  exactly that. On the command line, `cc-devthrottle message request` takes `--amount`, and
  `cc-devthrottle message link answer` takes `--approve`.
- Teams: you can create a team from the Cockpit's Account page, which now lists your teams and your role in each.
- On a Mac, the launcher repair reports every result to central error reporting - repaired, left alone and why, or
  failed - with your home folder and user name taken out.
- The Fleet Manager entry in the Cockpit's left menu no longer shows a red count.
- Factories, where the factory area is turned on: the last talk is shown in the talking seat's own clock, and the
  "change - coming" label is gone from the Factories screen.

The Not delivered chip, the talk request badge, creating a team, the random schedule display and the Factories
changes are in the hosted Gateway. The download carries the Director's grey-suggestion fix, the Mac installer and
launcher repair, and the `cc-devthrottle` command line changes for schedules and talk requests.
