# DevThrottle v2.3.1

The session list now tells you what its colours mean, a finished report no longer looks like a brand-new
session, you can answer the Wingman from the Cockpit and the phone, and Macs and Linux machines finally
keep their launcher up to date by themselves.

## Your Mac keeps its own launcher up to date

Until this release, no launcher on a Mac or a Linux machine had ever updated itself. Those machines kept
whatever launcher their last install left behind, for as long as they ran, while their Director carried on
updating every week. One machine here had drifted five releases: a Director on 2.3.0 with a launcher still
on 2.1.0.

That is worse than it sounds, because the launcher is the part that installs the Director's update. A
machine on an old launcher keeps old behaviour no matter how many releases go by, and the last release
said as much in its own notes - "a launcher updates itself, so machines pick this up on their own" was
true only on Windows.

It is true everywhere now. A Mac or Linux machine downloads the new launcher like any other update, and
its Director installs it once it can confirm the new launcher came up and can be reached. If the new one
does not come up, the previous one is put back. There is nothing to run by hand and no step to remember:
a machine that is behind catches up on its own, including one that is behind right now.

**Windows is unchanged.** Same sequence, same checks, same behaviour as before.

## The session list explains its colours

There were eleven dot colours and nothing in the product said what any of them meant, so the only way to
learn one was to guess.

Under the ordering switch - on the Cockpit roster and on the phone - there is now a **What do the colours
mean?** legend. It lists every colour with its name, what a session wearing it is doing, and whether it is
asking for you. The swatches are painted from the palette itself, so the legend cannot drift from what you
are actually looking at.

## A finished report is no longer green

A session the Wingman judged finished was painted green - the same green a brand-new session wears for
"Ready". Two opposite meanings on one colour, and a finished session read as a new one.

Finished is now cyan. Green means brand-new and nothing else. Carrying on stays purple.

## Answer the Wingman from the Cockpit and the phone

When the Wingman judges that a session has stopped and is waiting on a choice, you can now answer it from
the Cockpit or your phone instead of going to the machine. Pick the option and it is sent.

It checks that the screen still looks the way it did when the question was asked, so an answer cannot land
on a session that has moved on since.

## A judged report calms the row

A session the Wingman has judged - finished, or carrying on by itself - no longer sits in the list shouting
for attention. The row calms down, drops below anything genuinely red, and stops being counted as needing
you. While a verdict is being formed the row says so, and a judged row shows the Wingman's own line instead
of "Needs you".

This only applies when the Wingman's verdicts are switched on for your account. With it off, nothing in the
list changes.

## Also in this release

A fix to our own deploy pipeline, which could refuse a good commit after two deploys of it were started
close together: it read its own earlier, cancelled attempt as a failed check on the code.
