## v2.22.0 - October 16, 2026

A factory defined on the Gateway now runs from its definition alone: each seat starts on its machine with everything it needs downloaded, a check can decide whether a seat starts at all, and the Cockpit shows the definition and the factory's records.

### Highlights
- A seat starts from its definition. Where the factory area is turned on, a factory with a published definition
  starts each seat from what the Director downloads for it: the seat's brief, the factory's files, its rules, its
  skills, the secrets it names, and the agent and model the definition sets. The seat works in a fresh worktree of
  its repository, or in its own folder when it has none, and nothing of the factory is written into a checkout or
  into your own skills folder. A start the Director cannot make is refused with its reason, shown on the schedule.
- A check can decide whether a seat starts. A start can carry a small Python check, held in the definition and run
  by the Director on the seat's machine with no model. When the check finds nothing, no session starts and the run
  is recorded as nothing to do. When it finds work, the seat starts with what the check found. A check that fails
  is recorded with its reason and shown on the factory's problems card and in the morning email, never silently.
- The Definition and Records tabs. A factory's page in the Cockpit has a Definition tab, in place of Documents: an
  overview of the definition, what a publish would change, publish, history, restore, export and import as a zip.
  A Records tab beside Memory shows each seat's journals, the working documents with their versions, and the
  attachments.

### Also in this release
- A publish never guesses which existing schedule a start belongs to: when two starts of a seat could match the
  same schedules, the publish is refused and says which starts to tell apart.
- A seat page of a defined factory offers only the actions the Gateway allows, and the confirm before a publish or
  a restore names every other change that goes live with it.
- The schedule list shows schedules that come from a definition, and an ask rule in a definition carries a warning,
  because a scheduled seat has no one to answer it.
- The Cockpit's Sessions filter can filter on repositories and lists their worktrees under them.
