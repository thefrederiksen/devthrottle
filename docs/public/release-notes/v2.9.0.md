# DevThrottle v2.9.0

Restarting DevThrottle no longer throws away what your sessions were doing. They hand over, the
Director restarts, and it offers them back.

## Smart Restart

Until now, restarting the Director meant every running session simply ended. Whatever a session was in
the middle of was lost, and you rebuilt it by hand.

**File, Smart Restart** now shuts your sessions down nicely instead. Each one is asked to write a short
handover of what it was doing and what is left, the Director keeps a record of what was running, and then
it restarts. Closing the main window does the same thing and then stays closed. If no sessions are
running, neither door asks you anything.

Before anything happens you choose how long the sessions get - 5, 10, 15, 30 or 60 minutes, 10 by
default - for the whole shutdown, not per session. The time is a ceiling: the moment every session has
handed over, the restart goes ahead. Two thirds of the way through, any session still in the middle of a
turn is interrupted and asked to hand over now. A progress screen shows every session and what has
happened to it, with **Shut down now** to stop waiting and **Cancel and keep working** to call the whole
thing off - any session already closed is brought back from the handover it has just written.

One session that cannot take a prompt no longer holds up the rest. It is named on the screen and in the
record, and every other session is still asked.

If your computer is shutting down there is no time to ask anything, so DevThrottle writes down what was
running and lets the sessions end with it.

## Getting your sessions back

When the Director starts again it offers the restart back: **A restart is available**, with when it was,
the reason you gave, and a list of what can be brought back. Nothing comes back unless you say so.

- **Bring back** starts the sessions you ticked, leads first, each one reading its own handover.
- A session that ended without a handover - it ran out of time, or never answered - has a line of its own,
  offering to reopen its saved conversation, or, when its agent cannot do that, a fresh session in the
  same repository. The line says which one you would get before you press it.
- **Not now** asks you again next time.
- **Don't ask again** stops the offer for that restart. It brings nothing back and deletes nothing.

You are offered a restart once. After you have brought anything back from it, it is not offered again
when the Director starts, and a restart more than seven days old is not offered either. Nothing is ever
deleted: **File, Restart history** lists every restart this Director has done, newest first, says what
became of each session, and still offers whatever is left.

The record is kept on the Gateway, not on your computer, so it is still there if the machine is not.

## The Cockpit's Chat tab reads like a chat

Your own words now sit on the right in a narrow bubble, and the agent's answer takes the full width with
no card around it. **My prompts only** collapses a long conversation to just what you asked, and clicking
one of your prompts takes you straight back to that point in the full conversation.

## Also in this release

- **The Wingman says when it could not read a session.** A failed reading used to leave a stopped session
  silent with nothing on the card to say why. The card now says so, and the Gateway asks again on its own.
- **Open a session in the Cockpit from the Director.** A button at the end of the tab row goes straight to
  that session's Cockpit screen, where Voice mode and the Wingman live.
- **One list of repositories, in one order, everywhere.** The Director's new-session dialog, the Cockpit
  and the phone all read the same list, ordered once, and the list forgets folders that no longer exist.
- **Usage limits are recognised.** The Director now recognises the words agents really print when they
  stop on a usage limit. It used to look for a phrase no agent prints.
- **The session rail no longer shows a false alarm** for a colour an older Director has never heard of;
  it shows a neutral colour, and the rail has a colour legend.
- **`cc-cleanup-storage` can now reclaim space.** `reclaim` moves what a rule recommends into a holding
  folder on the same disk - nothing is deleted - and only with `--apply`; without it, it shows what it
  would do. `holding list`, `holding restore` and `holding purge` look at, put back, or finally remove
  what is being held. Four more Windows rules join the six from v2.8.1.
- **`cc-devthrottle session report` is now `session handback`**, which is what it does: it tells the
  session that started you what you did. The old name still works.
