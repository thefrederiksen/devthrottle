DevThrottle Terminology

Every name: what the thing is, what we call it today, what it should be called · reviewed independently by Codex and Grok · 2 August 2026

Twenty names, all settled. The rule we worked to: we do not have to copy the industry, but we must not use a word that already means something different in it. Two things still need building rather than deciding — the Task record, and the renames listed in the Cost column.

Where "team" is used loosely today, tighten it. The fleet preamble, the broadcast policy, the fleet-comms skill, the mission conduct and the command line all say "your own team" to mean the sessions you may message — which the skill defines as the sessions on your mission, or, for a solo session, the same repository. That second case is a team with no Team workflow. Where those strings mean message scope, say "the sessions on your mission"; keep "team" for the real thing.

OPEN, raised by the 14 September 2026 consolidation: "seat" now means three things. (1) A ROLE a session holds - "the Manager seat" - which this table already settles as Role. (2) A session's membership of a workflow run, which the Participation row above is retiring the word from. (3) An entry in a WORKSPACE - a repository plus the agent, model, role and owner that would make one session - which is not a role or a participation at all, and has no other name. The architecture documents use all three. This is the owner's call and is deliberately NOT resolved here; resolving it silently across three documents would be one session inventing vocabulary the fleet has to live with.

Owner was added on 14 September 2026, after this table was settled on 2 August. The ownership model shipped in between - a session-initiated spawn must now declare who will own the result - and it needed a word that covers the case where the answer is the USER, which "supervisor" cannot. Owner, Supervisor and Parent are three different questions, and the distinction is the whole of sessions.html section 1: who does it ANSWER TO, which SESSION is that, and who STARTED it. Only the first decides anything.

Mission and Run are two records, and that is correct. I originally wanted to collapse them into one. That was wrong. A Mission is a durable statement of purpose that holds several tasks and can outlive any single execution; a Run is one mechanical execution of a workflow. They link — one to one in the common case — and neither is a duplicate of the other.

Worker is a known collision we are choosing to accept. In Temporal and Kubernetes a worker is a process, not a role — both researchers flagged it, proposing Builder and Implementer. It is in the shipped workflow steps, the role constant, the mission conduct, the command line and the fleet preamble, and unlike "controller" it is a word we say out loud. Recorded as accepted rather than silently ignored. If we ever change it, Builder is the better alternative.

Five roles, final: Standalone, Architect, Manager, Worker, Reviewer. An Inspector is a Reviewer from a different agent family — the word stays in our conduct, the constraint is proved by the recorded agent kinds, and it is not a sixth role.

Two defects found on the way

Not names to choose — things to fix.

A mission's WHY is attached to its name, not its identity. The why lives in its own table, keyed by the mission's lower-cased name, while everything else about a mission joins on its id. Rename a mission and its why is silently orphaned; two missions sharing a name share one why and overwrite each other. For a field that is a standing rule — no why, no mission — that is the weakest possible attachment. One migration to fix.

The mission workflow contradicts itself about who inspects. One record, two answers: its conduct names the Inspector seven times and makes it load-bearing, while its five structured steps never mention it and say the Architect reviews the Manager. The conduct is the true one — it is what agents actually read at run time, and it is fidelity-tested. The steps should be fixed to match.
