## Finding

1. **High — a delayed first capture callback can leave the ready tone in the transcribed clip.** `src/CcDirector.Avalonia/Voice/BatchDictationRecorder.cs:550-555` records B0 as the byte count in the first callback and T0 when that callback arrives. `:334-342` then blanks only through B0 + bytes(T1 − T0) + 50 ms. This assumes the first callback arrived no more than one 50 ms buffer after those bytes were captured; the code does not measure that lag. `MicAudioCapture.cs:194-199` explicitly records that the capture thread can stall for `NumberOfBuffers × BufferMilliseconds` before the driver runs out of buffers. The installed NAudio 2.2.1 `WaveInEvent` defaults to **three** buffers (confirmed by constructing one and reading the property), giving 150 ms of buffer headroom at this capture's 50 ms buffer size. For example, if the first 50 ms buffer arrives 100 ms after it fills, the cue starts at audio position 150 ms, playback ends at 350 ms and ring-down ends at 400 ms, but the boundary is 50 + 200 + 50 = **300 ms**. The last 50 ms of the tone and 50 ms of ring-down reach transcription. `BatchDictationRecorderReadyCueTests.cs:194-214` tests a 50 ms first-buffer delay only; the zero-to-three-buffer cases vary the backlog at playback end, not the first callback's lag. `evidence/cue_to_speech_gap.py` measures a different quantity, the time from acoustic cue end to apparent speech, so it does not establish the assumed 50 ms callback bound. H1 removed the margin as ruled and protects early speech in the zero-lag case, but its stated maximum residual cue duration is not supported, and #2925's required silence through cue playback can fail under a capture stall within the driver's buffer headroom.
