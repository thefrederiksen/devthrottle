# Dictation Fixes - inspection 6

**Verdict: FAIL. Three high-severity findings and one low-severity finding.** I inspected the phase 6 commits from `afbbc864` onward and the full `origin/main...origin/mission/dictation-fixes` diff against devthrottle issues #2925-#2929. I also inspected `devthrottle_internal` branch `mission/dictation-fixes-docs` against internal issue #2038. I did not use `REPORT.md` as evidence.

## Findings

1. **High - a partly played cue can erase more of the owner's first word than the accepted dropped-buffer limit.** `src/CcDirector.Core/Audio/ReadyCue.cs:133-143` accepts a full 200 ms template match when only the cue's high-energy beginning is present. It then blanks the full 200 ms plus 50 ms ring-down. `src/CcDirector.Avalonia/Voice/DesktopAudioCue.cs:76-103` can stop playback with an error without reporting completion, but `src/CcDirector.Avalonia/Voice/BatchDictationRecorder.cs:296-316,363-386,410-435` merely waits for the three-second allowance and still runs this search. I ran `ReadyCue.Find` on 24 kHz PCM with the cue synthesised at the player's actual 44.1 kHz and resampled to the capture rate. It starts at 150 ms, stops early after **50 ms**, and has 20% of the generated cue's amplitude in the recording. I placed **200 ms of real, VAD-detected speech** from the local corpus clip `dictation-20260831-221542-13bff2ff.wav` (at its 512 ms speech onset, unscaled RMS -30.5 dBFS) **after** a 50 ms ring-down, at 250 ms. The score was **0.6235**; the match started at 150 ms and the recorder's blank span would be **140-400 ms**, erasing the first **150 ms of that speech**. With 100 ms of playback and a synthetic voice-like signal after ring-down, the score was still **0.5203** and the first 100 ms was blanked. No capture buffer was dropped. Both cases exceed the accepted limit of one 50 ms dropped buffer and violate #2925's after-cue preservation. The tests cover a complete cue with a missing end report (`BatchDictationRecorderReadyCueTests.cs:238-250`) and say a half cue is not found (`:303-306`), but never test one. A playback error after a partial output is the missing production case.

2. **High - the 'cue played' gate is set before playback starts, so an output failure can cause speech to be blanked when no cue sounded.** `src/CcDirector.Avalonia/Voice/SpeakDialog.axaml.cs:677-678` calls `NoteReadyCuePlaying` before `PlayReadyCue`; `DesktopAudioCue.cs:63-103` starts playback later on a worker thread and swallows initialization and playback failures. The recorder therefore searches and blanks on a recorder for which playback may never have started, contrary to ruling R5. The committed real-audio middle control itself gives a counterexample to treating a score above 0.5 as proof in this state: `dictation-20260907-134605-d11774d4.wav` scores **0.509** at 38.136 s, with a speech-detector span through 38.272 s overlapping the match. If that 1.5 s of recorded audio occurs at the beginning of a turn whose output device fails, the product search blanks a 260 ms span, including about **147 ms of detected speech**. The corpus establishes a possible false match, not its frequency at the beginning of failed-playback turns. `NoCuePlayed_ACueShapedSoundIsNotBlanked` (`BatchDictationRecorderReadyCueTests.cs:256-263`) manually leaves the gate unset; the dialog always sets it before attempting production playback. `CuePlayed_SpeechLikeSoundInTheSearchWindow_IsNotBlanked` (`:280-287`) uses one synthetic sound chosen to score below threshold. Neither covers this path.

3. **High - closing the Wake Word test dialog during its new background device query can leave a microphone recording after the window is gone.** `src/CcDirector.Avalonia/Voice/WakeWordTestDialog.axaml.cs:100-104` now awaits `Task.Run(DescribeDevice)` before it constructs and publishes `_recorder`. The close handler at `:62,184-188` disposes only the current `_recorder`, which is still null while the query is pending. When the query finishes, `BtnStart_Click` has no closed-window guard: it creates a recorder, starts the microphone, and assigns it to `_recorder` after the one close event has already run. That live capture has no remaining owner to stop or dispose it. The asynchronous gap was introduced by the #2929 constructor update; the Speak dialog has close guards, but this changed caller does not. A slow device query and a quick close suffice. The Avalonia suite has no test that holds this query while closing the Wake Word test dialog.

4. **Low - the internal research header misstates the size of the correction.** `devthrottle_internal/docs/research/transcription/2026-09-09-unspoken-words.md:8-10` says the entire "doubt" group was the ready cue. Its corrected section 7 says **fourteen openings** were the cue, while two other oracle-omitted segments and six partial matches remain doubt for other reasons. A reader of the opening summary could relabel all 22 as inventions or misread the unchanged scoring table. The header needs the same fourteen-of-22 qualification already present in the body and architecture document.

## Phase 6 checks

- The waveform generator is shared: `DesktopAudioCue.BuildWaterDropBloop` calls `ReadyCue.SynthesizePcm16`. The player generates 44.1 kHz mono PCM; `MicAudioCapture` captures 24 kHz, 16-bit mono PCM; the recorder generates its template at that capture rate. The C# score implements the research's zero-mean, unit-norm template and window-energy denominator. Its integer squared-energy prefix cannot overflow within the 1.55 s production search region. Search bounds and blanking are clamped to complete PCM16 samples; an odd trailing byte is ignored by detection and left untouched. Production's declared PCM16 mono capture uses whole-sample buffers.
- The successful-playback Send path waits after the 250 ms capture tail and before stopping the microphone, bounded by the player's three-second completion allowance. The stop drain precedes the snapshot. The completion report does not position the blank. The two failure paths above remain.
- I ran the C# product detector again on all **44** local corpus WAV files (all 24 kHz mono). Its output was byte-for-byte identical to the committed `cue_detector_corpus.csharp.json`. I ran `cue_detector_corpus.py` against those clips and the C# output; its `cue_detector_corpus.json` SHA-256 was unchanged. Research and product each found **30/44**, with no different found clips and no match-position difference over 1 ms; the three pre-cue May clips were negative. Both middle controls found **1/44**. None of the 30 blank spans overlapped Silero-detected speech; the minimum measured gap from blank end to the next detected speech was **129.5 ms**. This detector is an indirect speech measure and does not prove every word onset on future devices or during playback errors.
- I timed `ReadyCue.Find` five times on this machine in Release with a **60 s**, 24 kHz PCM clip, a cue at 150 ms and the production 1.55 s search end: **49.9, 44.5, 47.7, 45.3, 44.7 ms** (median **45.3 ms**, after one warm-up call). The search cost depends on the bounded opening region; copying and WAV wrapping the whole clip were outside this timing.
