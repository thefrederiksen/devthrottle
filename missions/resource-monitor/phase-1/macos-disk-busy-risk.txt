WHAT THE FOUR PHASES SHOW

1. THE PROBE'S THROUGHPUT AND OPERATION COUNTS AGREE WITH THE OPERATING SYSTEM'S OWN TOOL.
   Read side by side above, for the same windows:

       phase 1    probe  366.9 MiB/s,  3190 operations a second
                  iostat 365 to 374 MB/s,  3130 to 3316 transfers a second
       phase 2    probe 1711.2 MiB/s,  2170 operations a second
                  iostat 1554 to 1968 MB/s,  1711 to 2619 transfers a second
       phase 3    probe 1419.9 MiB/s,  2002 operations a second
                  iostat 1080 to 1871 MB/s,  1497 to 2545 transfers a second

   iostat averages over its own one second ticks while the probe averages over the whole window, so
   the two cannot be identical; they are the same figures. The bytes, the operation counts and the
   per-operation times coming out of IOKit are therefore sound, and the mission's disk read and write
   rates can be built on them.

2. THE "BUSY PERCENTAGE" IS NOT A PERCENTAGE. It is the average number of operations in flight,
   multiplied by a hundred, and it runs away above 100 whenever the disk is doing more than one thing
   at a time:

       idle                                      0.31%     queue depth  0.00
       one writer, flushing every block         17.23%     queue depth  0.17
       one writer, going flat out              234.11%     queue depth  2.34
       four writers, contended                2453.99%     queue depth 24.54

   The identity was checked directly: operations a second multiplied by milliseconds an operation
   reproduces the figure to within five percent on every load measured. So the mission's proposed
   limits of "above 80 percent for 60 seconds" for amber and 95 for red CANNOT be evaluated against
   this counter on macOS. Phase 2 is one program copying a file at full speed - a perfectly healthy
   machine - and it reads 234 percent, which is red twice over. Capping the figure at 100 would not
   rescue it, because the ordering is wrong rather than the scale.

   Supporting evidence that the platform simply does not offer utilisation: macOS's own iostat
   reports kilobytes per transfer, transfers a second and megabytes a second, and NO busy column at
   all. Linux's iostat has one; macOS's does not, because the figure is not available to report.

3. WHAT DOES TRACK SATURATION IS MILLISECONDS AN OPERATION, cleanly and in the right order:

       idle                                      0.000 ms per write
       one writer, flushing every block          0.052 ms per write
       one writer, going flat out                1.099 ms per write
       four writers, contended                  15.785 ms per write

   That is a monotonic, physically meaningful signal, and it is the same figure the mission's own
   evidence from devlinux used when it named the cause: "sdc, 75% busy, 26 ms per write". The
   milliseconds are the part of that sentence which means the same thing on all three platforms.

THE RULING TAKEN, AND BY WHOM

Reported to the phase 1 Tech Lead on 2026-10-15 with a recommendation; accepted the same day, all
three parts:

  - PhysicalDiskReading.BusyPercent becomes double? and is NULL on macOS, because the platform does
    not offer a utilisation figure and the mission says a figure the platform does not offer is null,
    never invented and never zero.
  - QueueLength is replaced by double? AverageQueueDepth, which is what this counter honestly
    measures, filled from total time over the interval.
  - The slowness diagnosis rests the macOS disk verdict on MillisecondsPerRead and
    MillisecondsPerWrite rather than on a percentage.

WHAT THIS PROOF DOES NOT COVER

  - ONE MACHINE, ONE DISK, ONE KIND OF DISK. This is an Apple M4 with a single internal solid-state
    disk. No spinning hard drive was measured on macOS at all, so the Rotational branch of the medium
    type is covered only by a committed fixture and by the code path, not by hardware. The mission's
    spinning-disk evidence is from devlinux, a different platform.
  - NO EXTERNAL OR NETWORK VOLUME was attached, so a disk appearing and disappearing while the probe
    is sampling is untested here.
  - THE READ SIDE IS UNDER-LOADED. These phases are write-heavy; the read figures above are whatever
    the machine happened to be doing. Reads were saturated separately during the investigation, at
    about 900 mebibytes a second for one reader with the file cache turned off and about 2765 for
    four, giving busy figures of 260 and 380 percent - the same conclusion by the same arithmetic.
  - THE LIMITS THEMSELVES ARE STILL PROPOSALS. This says what the macOS disk figure means, not what
    the right threshold on milliseconds per write is. That needs the phase 2 load harness on all
    three platforms, as the mission document already says.
